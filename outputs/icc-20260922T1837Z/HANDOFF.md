## Setup

`setup/probe.sh` surveyed the login node, partitions and tools, and
`setup/fetch.sh` fetched the pinned upstream sources there. `setup/build.sbatch`
(resumed from the TreePIR step by `setup/build-resume.sbatch`) built HEXL, the
shared exact-EP6 and ep3-s32 OnionPIR builds, SEAL, TreePIR's VBPIR and
SealPIR backends and Respire on a pool node, each with its correctness gate.

## Jobs

One script per job under `jobs/`; `pool.tsv` maps the jobs to their Slurm ids,
and the first Ours run (`primary`) was job 10693014. Every job took a whole
node of the `secondary` partition with the constraint
`G8358_100g_256G_NoGPU&intel` (ccc0400-ccc0415), checked the CPU model, CPU
count, NUMA layout and memory, ran the Standard 1 GiB calibration row on the
shared EP6 build and pinned one thread to CPU 0. The first runs used the shared
builds; the reruns and the Spiral, KsPIR and VIA jobs built their binaries on
their own node. The report's provenance table lists each job's node and source
commit.

- `primary`, `core-slf`, `core-merged` and `core-cuckoo` are the first runs,
  with the OnionPIRv2 baselines on exact EP6; `primary-v2` and the `-ep3` jobs
  rerun them on one ep3-s32 build, and the `-8g` jobs add 8 GiB.
- `kspir-pbc` first ran as 10696075 and died after its GCC build: Intel's
  `vars.sh` reads an unset variable, which ends a shell under `set -u`. The
  scripts now wrap it in `set +u`; that job and the five other icpx jobs were
  resubmitted as 10735511-10735517. The failed run is kept as
  `runs/kspir-pbc-failed-10696075` for its calibration row.
- `via-b` and `via-b-8g` were resubmitted once before running, when
  `run_via_matrix.py --scheme` replaced `run_via_b_matrix.py --batch-mode`.
- `treepir-vbpir` tried VBPIR at 4 and 8 GiB first; both failed with a zero
  noise budget (72 and 138 GiB peak), so its smaller sizes were not run and
  the TreePIR row is `treepir` plus `treepir-8g` on SealPIR d=2.
- Spiral + PBC and Respire were not attempted at 8 GiB: their 4 GiB peaks
  put 8 GiB at about 336 and 400 GiB against 251 GiB per node.
